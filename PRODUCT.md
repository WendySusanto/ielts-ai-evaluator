# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

The primary user is an IELTS candidate preparing for the test — often a non-native English speaker studying on their own, under real time and score pressure, who needs to know where they actually stand and what to fix next. They arrive anxious and outcome-focused: the goal is a specific target band, not general "better English."

A secondary audience is the internal admin/ops team who manage speaking and writing prompts, review users, and curate evaluation content. Their surface (the Admin area) is a real product surface with its own task flow, not an afterthought — but candidate-facing screens set the tone and take priority in any trade-off.

Because most users are ESL, interface language itself is part of the product: copy, labels, and errors must read plainly to someone still learning English.

## Product Purpose

An AI-powered IELTS practice and evaluation platform for writing and speaking. Users submit essays (Task 1 / Task 2) or speaking responses and receive detailed, criterion-based feedback — Task Achievement/Response, Coherence and Cohesion, Lexical Resource, and Grammatical Range and Accuracy — scored the way an examiner would, plus a progress view that tracks band trends across attempts.

Success is a user reaching their target band. Everything else — retention, conversion to Premium — follows from feedback that is trusted and acted on. The product wins when a candidate practices, understands the gap, closes it, and sees the band move.

## Positioning

Feedback mapped to the official IELTS band descriptors and scored like an examiner would — not the generic "make your writing better" advice of a general grammar checker or an open-ended chatbot. The strategic claim every screen reinforces: *this scores you the way the real test does.*

## Brand Personality

Calm, credible, encouraging. The voice is a supportive expert examiner: authoritative enough to be trusted with a score, warm enough that the feedback lands as help rather than judgment. IELTS prep is high-stakes and stressful, so the interface should lower anxiety, not add to it — reassurance carried through restraint and clarity, confidence carried through precision. Never hype, never cutesy, never cold.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI-chatbot SaaS**: purple gradients, "AI magic" sparkles, hero-metric templates, the interchangeable LLM-wrapper look. The credibility here is IELTS-grade assessment, not novelty AI.
- **Cold, clinical exam software**: bureaucratic government-test-portal gray, joyless dense tables, stress-inducing officialdom.
- **Cluttered dashboard overload**: many widgets and charts competing on one screen, cognitive overload.

The user's own brief: simple, clean, minimalistic, informative but restrained — not too much information on one screen, not too many colors. Positive reference is **Tokopedia**: clean and approachable, organized so that even dense content reads as calm and scannable rather than busy.

## Design Principles

- **One screen, one task.** Show what the user needs for the job in front of them and nothing more. Progressive disclosure over dense dashboards; a calm, scannable surface over a wall of widgets.
- **Examiner-grade credibility.** Every score traces to an official IELTS criterion. Feedback is specific and evidenced, never vague praise — trust is the whole product.
- **Calm under pressure.** Design to lower test anxiety: reassuring tone, gentle motion, feedback framed as the next step forward rather than a verdict.
- **Show progress, not just corrections.** Make improvement visible over time so practice feels like it's compounding toward the target band.
- **Plain language for ESL users.** Copy, labels, and errors read clearly to someone still learning English. Clarity beats cleverness everywhere.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: AA contrast ratios, full keyboard navigation, screen-reader labels on interactive elements, and a reduced-motion path for every animation. Beyond the standard, treat ESL accessibility as a first-class requirement — plain-language UI copy, unambiguous labels, and no idiom-heavy or culturally-loaded phrasing that a non-native English speaker would stumble on.
